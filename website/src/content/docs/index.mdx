Valet is a homepage for your homelab that can also do things.

## The front door to your homelab

Instead of opening five different tabs to work out what's going on, just open Valet. It shows what's happening across all your services, and you can act on it right there. Instead of just being a page with links and a status dot, Valet is meant to be powerful and actionable.

<figure class="why"><img src="/why/front-door.webp" alt="Valet's main screen: the greeting at the top left with a one-line summary of what's wrong and what's playing, an ask box, every service's status, then the 3D house, the PC and a week of the calendar side by side" loading="lazy" /></figure>

## AI-native, if you want it

Ask Valet anything about your homelab. Use a local model or a hosted one. Valet knows its own documentation, so it can help you set itself up! Anything it changes waits for your approval, and Valet works just fine with the AI switched off.

## Updates in real time

Everything on the page is live. Progress bars move, numbers tick over, and new things appear as they happen, with no refreshing. Hover anything in the top bar for its details: what's playing, a download, the CPU or the weather.

## Wall display mode

Open [`/wall`](/concepts/wall/) on a tablet or a spare monitor for a page made to be read from across the room. The wall is its own page: you pick exactly which cards it shows, several pages can take turns, and it dims at night. Nothing on it can be pressed, and chat never shows.

## Flexible, and light

One YAML file controls everything, with [185 integrations](/integrations/) and a simple way to [add your own](/build/). It runs as one Node process, uses about 45 MB of RAM on a fresh install, and the image is 165 MB.

<figure class="why"><img src="/why/config-live.webp" alt="Editing config.yaml while Valet runs, ten times over: the greeting, the wallpaper, liquid glass, light mode, the greeting's place, a labelled dock at the bottom, two columns, the full services list, no ask box and a serif font, each on the page as soon as the line is saved" loading="lazy" /></figure>

## Security first

Valet is read-only until you turn on sign-in or tell it your network is private. Keep your API keys in `.env` and they stay on the server, so neither the browser nor the AI ever sees them, and the assistant only gets the access you give it, one integration at a time. Anything the assistant changes asks first. It runs on your own hardware, with no account and no telemetry. The [security docs](/security/) cover the rest.
