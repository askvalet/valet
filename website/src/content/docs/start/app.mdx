Valet can sit on your Home Screen and in your dock like any other app, and it can send notifications to that device itself. You don't need ntfy, Telegram or anything else for that.

<figure class="why"><img src="/why/app-notifications.webp" alt="⌘K with Notifications on this device under This device, then Ask and the docs about notifications" loading="lazy" style="max-width:520px" /></figure>

## What it needs

- **HTTPS.** Browsers only install apps and allow notifications from a secure address. Put Valet behind your reverse proxy with a certificate (see [sign-in and network safety](/start/sign-in/)). `http://localhost` also counts, which is handy for trying it. A plain `http://` address on your network, like `http://10.0.0.5:7575`, doesn't.
- **A page that may act**, for notifications. Turning them on changes something, so it needs what a button needs: sign-in, or `auth.trustNetwork` on a private network. Installing works either way.

## Install it

- **iPhone and iPad:** open Valet in Safari, tap **Share**, then **Add to Home Screen**.
- **Android:** open Valet in Chrome, open the menu and tap **Add to home screen** or **Install app**.
- **Chrome and Edge on a computer:** click the install icon at the end of the address bar.
- **Safari on a Mac:** **File**, then **Add to Dock**.

It opens in its own window, without the browser's bars, and takes your config's `title` as its name once you're signed in.

## Turn on notifications

On the device, open ⌘K (the search bar on a phone), type **notifications** and pick **Notifications on this device**. The browser asks for permission only then. A first notification arrives straight away, so you know it works.

Pick it again to turn them off. Signing out from ⌘K turns them off on that device too.

**On iPhone and iPad, add Valet to the Home Screen first** and turn notifications on from the app there. Apple only allows notifications for web apps on the Home Screen. In Safari, the switch says so.

## What you're told

Everything that already goes to your [notification channels](/recipes/#notifications) goes to these devices too:

- reminders ("tell me when this finishes")
- [rules](/assistant/rules/) when they run, or can't
- notices in `live.notify` with `push: true`
- the assistant's shared-cause line, with `notify.insights: true`

A notification opens Valet on the tab that holds the widget it's about. Something urgent, like a service going down, stays on screen until you deal with it.

## Settings

Nothing to set for most people. It's on by default, and it sends nothing until a device turns it on.

```yaml
notify:
  webPush:
    enabled: true                         # false: no device can turn them on, and nothing is sent
    contact: mailto:you@example.com       # how a push service can reach you; defaults to https://askvalet.dev
```

`services` lists the push services Valet will post to. Browsers use Google's, Apple's, Mozilla's and Microsoft's, which are the default, so you'd only add to it for a push server of your own. See [the reference](/reference/configuration/#notify).

## Offline

Valet installs a small service worker. When the server can't be reached, the app says **Valet can't be reached** instead of the browser's error page, and that's all it keeps. It never shows an old copy of the page or your data, so nothing is out of date, and nothing private stays on the device. See [what's kept and where](/security/#notifications-and-the-app).

## The demo

The [demo](https://demo.askvalet.dev) installs like the real thing, but it has no server to send notifications from. Its switch explains that.

## If it doesn't work

- **The switch says the page came over plain HTTP:** put Valet on HTTPS.
- **Notifications are blocked:** you said no to the browser's question once. Allow them in the site's settings and pick the switch again.
- **Nothing arrives on Android:** check that battery saving isn't stopping the browser in the background.
- **The server log says `push to … answered 403`:** the push service refused Valet's signature. Check the server's clock, then turn notifications off and on again on that device.
